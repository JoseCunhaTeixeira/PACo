PACo's atlas, dispersion images, as look draws them: phase velocity against frequency, each
point's coherence from dark (0) to yellow (1), the automatic pick in white dots, λ = 2·dx dashed in
red (under it a ridge may be an alias) and λ = 3·L dotted in red (left of it, beyond the window's
reach). From PAC's test lines: what each case looks like, and what it asks.

- 1. Clean (passive-active, 11 receivers, a mute of 50 to 1,500 m/s): one sharp ridge, continuous
  over a wide band, rising toward the low frequencies, the rest dark; the pick on its crest. The
  image to aim for.
- 2. Stripes (the same windows, unmuted): vertical bands across the whole image and a ridge broken
  into pieces: every arrival of the shots correlated, the refractions and the noise with the
  surface waves. A mute first.
- 3. Smear (5 receivers): one broad bright area at the low frequencies and no ridge: a window this
  short resolves no wavelength much longer than itself (Δv/v ≈ λ/L). A longer window.
- 4. No ridge (active, 11 receivers): nothing stands above the rest, and the picker finds no curve:
  too little of the shots' energy at this window. Passive-active, more shots, or a reject.
- 5. A pick off the ridge (5 receivers): dots in a smear at 700 to 800 m/s, on no ridge. Not a
  curve: mend the image first, never accept it.
- 6. Aliases, and beyond reach (passive, 11 receivers): the thin bright ridges under the dashed line
  are aliases, not the ground; the bright area left of the dotted line lies beyond the window's
  reach. Neither is M0, and here the surface waves' ridge barely shows.
