PACo's atlas, dispersion images, as look and PAC draw them: phase velocity against frequency, each
frequency's row scaled from its least value (yellow) to its largest (black, edged in red), the
automatic pick in white dots, λ = 2·dx dashed in red (under it a ridge may be an alias) and λ = 3·L
dotted in red (left of it, beyond the window's reach). Scaled row by row, a weak column's peak is
as dark as a strong one's: the background tells how far it stands above the rest. From PAC's test
lines: what each case looks like, and what it asks.

- 1. Clean (passive-active, 11 receivers, a mute of 50 to 1500 m/s): one sharp dark ridge,
  continuous over a wide band, rising toward the low frequencies, the rest yellow; the pick on its
  crest. The image to aim for.
- 2. Stripes (the same windows, unmuted): vertical bands across the whole image and a ridge broken
  into pieces: every arrival of the shots correlated, the refractions and the noise with the
  surface waves. A mute first.
- 3. Smear (5 receivers): dark from the grid's bottom to its top under about 35 Hz, the ridge only
  above it (220 to 260 m/s, to 60 Hz): a window this short resolves no wavelength much longer than
  itself (Δv/v ≈ λ/L). That width is its resolution, not noise: a longer window sharpens it, and
  is better only where its ridge stays as unbroken at the higher frequencies.
- 4. A weak ridge (active, 11 receivers, unmuted): a thin ridge at 210 to 240 m/s from 40 to 60
  Hz, blobs from 250 to 400 m/s under 40 Hz, and the picker keeps two dots: too little of the
  shots' energy at this window (background 0.74). A mute, passive-active or other shots; a lasso
  on the thin ridge, or a reject.
- 5. A pick off the ridge (5 receivers): dots at 330 to 420 m/s in the dark smear, above the ridge
  near 250 m/s. Not a curve: mend the image first, never accept it.
- 6. Aliases, and beyond reach (passive, 11 receivers): the thin dark ridge under the dashed line
  is an alias, not the ground; the dark arc left of the dotted line lies beyond the window's
  reach. Neither is M0, and here the surface waves' ridge barely shows.
