# G3: curve QC, per window

Runs on each window's M0 pick, after the picking (an attempt of the `picking` stage in the QC
log, whose curve is saved in PAC's layout, `DispersionCurves_0000.csv`). Today's
`dispersion_quality` metrics, said in the gates' language, then the curve's own rules: the
wavelength range, mode jumps, the air wave, the trend, the points, the uncertainties. Code:
`src/paco/qc/g3_curve.py` over `paco.quality.measure_quality`; thresholds: `curve` in
`qc_config.json` (`CurveThresholds`, with today's `QualityParameters` under `metrics`); the
picking a run starts from: `picking` in the same file (`max_wavelength` 2 window lengths); tests:
`tests/test_qc_curve.py` and, for the metrics themselves, `tests/test_quality.py`.

## What it checks

The first four are medians over the pick's tracked points (the image's columns); the rest look
at the curve saved for the inversion, resampled in wavelength, sorted by wavelength.

| Metric | Threshold (default) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `sharpness` | peak width above the floor, over the width the window resolves (v x wavelength / window length): at least 0.8 | A perfect plane wave scores about 1; well below it, the peak is a fringe or an edge, not a propagating wave: rejected, no parameter helps at this window length |
| `prominence` | peak height above the floor over the column's median height: at least 2.0 | Barely above the rest of the image: filter to the band (G2's, or G1's usable one), or mute |
| `on_data` | share of points on their column's brightest value (within 10 %): at least 0.6 | The tracker's smoothing drew the curve, not the data: competing ridges; narrow the band |
| `constant_wavelength` | share of points where velocity grows like frequency: at most 0.4 | The edge of what the window resolves, not a dispersion curve: cut the long wavelengths (`max_wavelength` = 2 window lengths) |
| `n_points` | at least 2 | With no ridge kept: pick again with a looser mode rule (`mode_min_ratio` 1.2) |
| `long_wavelengths` | share of the curve's points beyond `max_wavelength_lengths` (2) window lengths: 0 | The window does not resolve them: cut (`max_wavelength` 2), and G3's `kept` counts only the points within |
| `aliased_points` | share of points below 2 x the receiver spacing: reported | The picker starts above it; a check that it did |
| `points_within` | at least `min_points` (5) within the range | Too few for a curve: keep more of the ridge (`min_relative_coherence` x 0.6) |
| `max_jump` | largest relative velocity step between consecutive points: at most 0.3 | Beyond it the pick jumped onto another mode: track again with half the corridor |
| `air_wave_share` | share of points at 330–345 m/s: at most 0.5 | The air wave, not the ground: mute the arrivals faster than 320 m/s (`muting {mute, 80, 320}`) |
| `trend` | Spearman rank correlation of velocity with wavelength: negative is flagged | Normal dispersion has velocity rising with wavelength; the opposite can be a stiff layer over a softer one: flagged and kept (`Keep`), never rejected |
| `uncertainty` | median Lorentzian uncertainty over the velocity: at most 0.5 | The array's resolving power (receivers, spacing), the same for every window of a line: beyond it the inversion cannot use the curve, and only longer windows help: rejected, not fixable |
| `near_offset` | the distance from the window's nearest shot to its receivers (from `window.json`): at least half the longest wavelength kept | The spec's near-offset rule: closer, the long wavelengths may read slow (near field). Reported and kept (`near_field`), never applied: keeping only far shots lost a third of the demo's curves (the user's decision of milestone 13, `S2_rules.md`) |

Verdict: `reject` when a flag is not fixable (sharpness, uncertainty), `retry` when every flag
can be acted on, `pass` with no flag or kept flags only. `kept`: the curve's band and wavelength
range within the limit, its points there (the resampled curve, 5 to 10 points for a 24-receiver
window: what the inversion gets, not the 110 to 150 tracked columns), the window's receivers.

## On analytic images (the tests)

- a clean ridge: pass, a resampled curve of about 23 points at 5 to 27 m, uncertainties 5 to
  50 %, the curve rules all passed;
- a ridge 0.3 times as wide as the window resolves: `sharpness`, rejected;
- a ridge 0.8 above a flat background of 0.6: `prominence` -> `filtering {iir, 12–38 Hz}`;
- a brighter ridge above M0: `on_data` -> `dispersion {fmin 10, fmax 40}` (the pick's band);
- a ridge at a constant 6 m wavelength: `constant_wavelength` -> `picking {max_wavelength 2}`;
- no pick: `no_ridge` -> `picking {mode_min_ratio 1.2}`;
- a curve with one point at 99 m for a 47 m window: `long_wavelengths` -> `max_wavelength 2`,
  kept 60 of 61 points;
- a curve 60 % faster from 30.5 Hz on: `mode_jump` -> `corridor 0.1`;
- a curve at 330 to 345 m/s: `air_wave` -> `muting {mute, 80, 320}`;
- a curve falling from 200 m/s at 20 m to 433 m/s at 11 m: `inverse_dispersion`, kept, pass;
- a curve of 3 points: `too_few_points` -> `min_relative_coherence 0.3`;
- uncertainties of 60 % of the velocity: `uncertain`, rejected;
- a shot 0.75 m from a window whose curve reaches 27.85 m: `near_field`, kept, pass; 20 m away,
  or no geometry given: nothing.

## To judge

- Sharpness and uncertainty reject without a retry: right for a window, or should the
  line-level decision (a longer window for the whole profile) be the action instead?
- The `prominence` fix filters to G2's band; when G2 found none, it mutes. Is a mute the
  better first move on active data?
- `max_jump` at 30 %: the good demo curves step by up to 26 % between consecutive points of a
  curve resampled every metre of wavelength, the bad ones by 31 and 53 %. Thin margin: is a
  jump better defined on the tracked points (every 0.5 Hz) than on the resampled curve?
- `min_points` at 5: the demo's curves keep 5 to 10 points at 2 window lengths. Is 5 enough
  for PAC's 2-layer inversion, or should the cut sit at 3 window lengths?
- The inverse trend on 11 of 19 windows at 2 to 11 m: geology (a stiff crust), or the picker
  favouring the short wavelengths' higher velocities? A figure per window would settle it; the
  flag is kept either way.
